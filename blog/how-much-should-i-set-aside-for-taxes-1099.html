How much should I set aside for taxes 1099?

How much should I set aside for taxes 1099? That is the question many US contractors ask once client payments arrive with nothing withheld. Freelancers in other countries ask the same thing in different words. There is no single correct percentage that works for every person, in every country, in every year. This article explains why, describes a few official building blocks in the United States and the United Kingdom, and shows how to use a rate you choose as a planning estimate. It is not tax advice, and it is not a calculation of what you will owe.

Why a single number does not exist

Tax on self-employment income usually depends on your profit (income minus allowable expenses), not on the face value of an invoice. It can also depend on other income, on allowances and deductions, on whether you owe social-security or National Insurance contributions, and on local or state taxes. Two freelancers who each invoice $80,000 can have very different amounts to pay.

That is why Invoice Pad’s tax set-aside helper asks you for a rate. The US and UK presets are starting points only. You can change the rate and the number of periods. The helper will not file anything, and it will not tell you your legal liability.

United States: more than income tax

If you work for yourself in the United States, you may owe income tax and self-employment tax on your net earnings from self-employment. The IRS explains that self-employment tax is a Social Security and Medicare tax for people who work for themselves. Under the rules described on the IRS self-employment tax page, the self-employment tax rate is 15.3 percent, made up of 12.4 percent for Social Security and 2.9 percent for Medicare. Social Security tax applies only up to the annual wage base, and an additional Medicare tax can apply above a threshold. Those figures can change; read the IRS page for the year you are planning.

Income tax sits on top of that and is graduated. State income tax may apply as well. You may also be able to deduct the employer-equivalent portion of self-employment tax, and you may have retirement contributions, health insurance, or other deductions that change the picture. None of that can be reduced to one unofficial “freelancer tax rate” without looking at your own return.

Because of this mix, some US freelancers choose a conservative planning percentage of profit that is high enough to cover income tax, self-employment tax, and a margin for state tax, then adjust it after a year of real figures or a conversation with an adviser. A mid-20s to around 30 percent starting point is a common planning habit, not a legal rate. If your profit is low, if you have large deductible expenses, or if you already have tax withheld elsewhere, a lower set-aside may be enough. If you have high earnings, a state tax, or few deductions, you may need more.

United Kingdom: income tax and National Insurance

UK sole traders usually pay Income Tax and National Insurance on their self-employed profits through Self Assessment. The rates, bands, and allowances are set each tax year and are published on GOV.UK Self Assessment and the pages for Income Tax and National Insurance. They are not repeated here because they change and because your band depends on all of your income, not only on freelance invoices.

If you have to make payments on account, you typically pay in two instalments, on 31 January and 31 July, with a possible balancing payment on 31 January after the tax year. Official guidance is on payments on account. If a date falls on a weekend or bank holiday, the deadline usually moves to the next working day; confirm that on GOV.UK for the year in question. A planning set-aside of around a quarter of profit is only a starting point for some people. It is not a substitute for a tax calculation.

A method you can repeat

Pick a period, such as a month or a quarter. Add up the income you actually received or earned in that period, according to the accounting basis you use. Subtract the business expenses you can support with records. Multiply the remaining profit by a rate you have chosen. Put that amount in a separate savings account, not in the same balance you spend from. Review the rate after you file, or after an adviser has looked at a year of figures.

If expenses are higher than income in a period, there is no profit in that estimate to reserve. That does not automatically mean you will owe nothing for the year. A later profitable quarter can change the annual result.

Quarterly habits in the US

Many US freelancers make estimated tax payments four times a year if they expect to owe tax above the IRS threshold for their situation. The payment periods are not four equal three-month blocks. For 2027 due dates, see US quarterly estimated tax dates for 2027. Setting money aside each time you are paid is easier than finding a lump sum on the due date.
