Quarterly estimated tax dates 2027

Quarterly estimated tax dates 2027 matter if you are a US freelancer who pays estimated tax and wants the calendar before the year starts. This page applies the Internal Revenue Service’s published payment periods and weekend-or-holiday rule to calendar year 2027. The IRS had not posted a 2027 Form 1040-ES at the time of writing, so you should confirm the dates on that form when it is issued, and on the IRS estimated tax FAQ.

This is a calendar for planning. It is not advice about whether you must pay estimated tax, or how much to pay. Those questions depend on your own tax position. Farmers, fishermen, fiscal-year taxpayers, and people who file and pay early in January can have different rules, which the IRS describes in Publication 505, Tax Withholding and Estimated Tax.

The IRS payment periods

For estimated tax, the year is not split into four identical quarters. The IRS payment periods and ordinary due dates are:

Income periodOrdinary due date
1 January to 31 March15 April
1 April to 31 May15 June
1 June to 31 August15 September
1 September to 31 December15 January of the following year

That table is from the IRS FAQ “When are quarterly estimated tax payments due?” Notice that the June payment covers only April and May, and that the January payment covers four months. If you set aside the same amount on a rigid three-month rhythm, you will not match the official periods.

The weekend and holiday rule

The same IRS FAQ says that if a due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the payment is on time if you make it on the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday. You still have to apply that rule to the calendar for the year in question.

Planning dates for 2027

Applying those ordinary dates and that rule to 2027 and early 2028 gives this planning calendar for a calendar-year individual:

InstallmentIncome periodPlanning due date
First1 January–31 March 202715 April 2027 (Thursday)
Second1 April–31 May 202715 June 2027 (Tuesday)
Third1 June–31 August 202715 September 2027 (Wednesday)
Fourth1 September–31 December 202718 January 2028 (Tuesday)

15 April, 15 June, and 15 September 2027 do not fall on a weekend. 15 January 2028 is a Saturday. The following Monday, 17 January 2028, is Martin Luther King Jr. Day, a federal legal holiday. Under the IRS rule above, the next day that is not a weekend or legal holiday is Tuesday, 18 January 2028. When Form 1040-ES for 2027 is published, use the dates printed there if they differ.

The January exception

The IRS notes an exception for the January payment. In broad terms, you may not have to make that installment if you file your return and pay the tax due by 31 January, subject to the details in Publication 505. Do not assume the exception applies until you have read that chapter and checked the weekday of 31 January in the relevant year.

How to use the dates as a freelancer

The due date is a payment deadline, not the first day you should think about tax. A practical habit is to move a planned amount into a separate account each time a client pays you, then send the estimated payment by the IRS due date. You can pay through the methods the IRS lists on its estimated taxes page, including payment options described under Make a payment.

If you miss a due date or pay too little, the IRS may charge a penalty even if you are due a refund when you file your annual return. That warning comes from the same FAQ. The way to reduce last-minute pressure is to treat the four dates as already known, and to keep a running record of profit, not only of invoices you have issued.

State deadlines are separate

Some states also require estimated payments, and their dates and thresholds are not always the same as the federal ones. This article covers federal Form 1040-ES dates only. Check your state tax agency for any additional calendar.
