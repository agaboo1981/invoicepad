What to include on an invoice

What to include on an invoice depends on where you and your client are, and on whether you charge VAT or sales tax. There is no single worldwide form. The lists below are a practical starting point for freelancers who invoice in the United States, the United Kingdom, or the European Union. They are not legal advice. Always follow the rules that apply to you, and read the official guidance linked here.

A useful core, wherever you work

Every freelance invoice should make five things obvious: who is billing, who is being billed, a unique invoice number, the date, and the amount due. Add a due date, a description of the work, quantities and unit prices, and how to pay. If you charge tax, show the rate and the tax amount. If a contract or purchase order exists, put that reference on the document so the buyer can match it.

United States

The United States does not issue a single federal “standard invoice” for freelance work. Your invoice still needs to be a clear business record for you and for the client. Include your name or business name, contact details, the client’s name, the invoice number and date, a description of services, the amount, and payment instructions. If you collect sales tax, show it in line with the rules of the state that applies to that sale.

Some clients will ask for a Taxpayer Identification Number on a Form W-9, or for an employer identification number on file, before they pay. That process sits beside the invoice; it is not a substitute for a numbered bill. At tax time, income from clients may be reported on information returns such as Form 1099-NEC when the thresholds and rules in force for that year are met. See the IRS page on reporting payments to independent contractors for the government’s description of that system. Your invoice file remains the day-to-day record of what you billed.

United Kingdom

GOV.UK sets out what invoices must include if you are VAT registered, and what is good practice if you are not. In summary, a VAT invoice needs a unique invoice number, the invoice date, your name or trading name and address, your VAT number, the customer’s name or trading name and address, a description of what you are selling, the date of supply if it is different from the invoice date, the quantity and unit price, the VAT rate, the amount of VAT, and the total. Official guidance is on GOV.UK: invoicing and taking payment from customers.

If you are not VAT registered, you should not charge VAT or show a VAT number. You should still issue a clear, numbered invoice with your details, the client’s details, the work, and the amount due. If you later register for VAT, keep the earlier invoices; they are part of your records. HMRC also expects you to keep business records for the period set out in self-employed record keeping.

European Union

EU VAT invoicing rules come from the VAT Directive and are applied by each member state. A full VAT invoice typically includes the date of issue, a unique sequential number, the supplier’s name, address and VAT identification number, the customer’s name and address, the customer’s VAT number when it is required (for example in some cross-border supplies), the quantity and nature of the goods or services, the date of the supply, the unit price, any discounts, the VAT rate, the VAT amount, and the taxable amount. Some member states allow simplified invoices below a threshold. For an official overview, see the European Commission page on VAT in the European Union and your own tax authority’s invoicing list.

If a supply is reverse-charged, the invoice usually has to say so, in the wording your member state requires, rather than showing VAT as if you had charged it in the ordinary way. If you are not VAT registered, do not invent a VAT number or a VAT line. When you invoice a client in another country, check where the supply is treated as taking place; that question is separate from how you design the PDF.

Currency, language and format

Invoice in the currency you agreed in the contract. If you and the client use different currencies, say which currency the amount is in and, if you have agreed a conversion method, mention it in the notes. Use a language the client’s accounts team can read. A PDF is the usual format because it does not reflow when someone else opens it.

Electronic invoices in some EU public-sector contracts must follow a structured format, not only a PDF. If you bid for that work, ask the buyer which standard they accept. A browser-generated PDF is still suitable for most private freelance work.

A short checklist before you send

Check the names, the invoice number, the dates, the line items, the tax treatment, the total, and the payment details. Check that any purchase order is present. Check that you have a copy. If you are unsure whether you must charge VAT or sales tax, that is a question for your adviser or the official guidance above, not for a template to guess on your behalf.
